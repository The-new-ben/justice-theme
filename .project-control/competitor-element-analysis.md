## din.co.il

Homepage structure:
- Directory and legal content are tightly mixed: lawyer discovery, article/news blocks, forum answers, practice areas.
- Search intent is broad but commercially oriented: users arrive with a legal problem and are moved toward a lawyer.

Header/menu:
- Practice areas are central. The model is category-first, then geography.

Search UX:
- Strong pattern: legal area + city + free text. Jus-Tice should keep this but make it cleaner and more premium.

Lawyer/provider cards:
- Cards function as conversion units: name, specialty, region, quick contact.

Profile pages:
- Need photo, firm, areas, city, contact, bio, and trust signals.

Trust signals:
- Forum answers by lawyers, long-running content library, visible breadth.

Monetization hints:
- Directory visibility and sponsored placement are likely core.

Ideas to adapt:
- Build practice x city paths, but avoid thin doorway pages.
- Add Q&A/forum as Phase 2 SEO moat.

What to avoid:
- Cluttered visual hierarchy and old directory feel.

## PsakDin

Homepage structure:
- More editorial/news authority than marketplace. Good for E-E-A-T inspiration.

Header/menu:
- Legal topics, forums, judgments/news, lawyer discovery.

Search UX:
- Public snippets show deep city link blocks by region. This is strong SEO architecture but can become noisy.

Article pages:
- Emphasize dates, authors, legal context, and case-law style content.

Trust signals:
- Legal news/case-law authority, professional tone, topic depth.

Ideas to adapt:
- Add article templates with author, reviewer, update date, sources, disclaimer, related lawyers.
- Build a serious legal magazine layer, not just "blog posts."

What to avoid:
- Editorial-only model with weak lead conversion.

## Justia

Homepage/category structure:
- Lawyer directory organized by practice area and location.
- Public page states lawyer profiles can include contact info, education, associations, practice areas, website/blog/social links.

Search UX:
- User can research, compare, and contact attorneys by legal issue and location.

Lawyer cards/profiles:
- Free profile claim is a growth mechanism.
- Premium placement increases visibility; Justia describes Platinum/Gold placements and profile traffic reporting.

Trust signals:
- Profile completeness, ratings/reviews, Q&A participation, professional metadata.

Monetization hints:
- Free profiles, premium placements, enhanced profiles, profile traffic reports.

Ideas to adapt:
- Free lawyer profile claim -> paid visibility.
- Traffic stats for lawyers.
- Profile completeness score.
- Do not call paid placement "recommendation"; label it as sponsored/promoted.

What to avoid:
- Copying US-specific breadth or legal claims that do not fit Israeli law/ethics.

## Midrag

Homepage/category structure:
- Marketplace trust is the product. Service/category pages push provider comparison and lead capture.

Trust signals:
- Public material highlights many reviews, quality control, phone verification, and provider removal when quality falls below threshold.

Search UX:
- Find provider by service/category/location and then compare.

Monetization hints:
- Provider network and quality mark.

Ideas to adapt:
- Verified review workflow later, but only with strict proof.
- Quality control language can inspire "profile completeness" and "documents reviewed" statuses.

What to avoid:
- Yelp-like legal ratings without compliance and moderation rules.

## Takdin / Legal Database Sites

Search UX:
- Takdin public text describes unified search across case law, legislation, articles, and more.

Ideas to adapt:
- Long-term legal database search layer: articles, forms, judgments summaries, lawyers.
- Add filters by source type and legal area.

What to avoid:
- Presenting scraped legal database content without rights/permissions.

## Product Implications For Jus-Tice

VERIFIED direction:
- Keep the homepage search-first.
- Make practice area and city the core navigation axes.
- Add lawyer cards and profile completeness, not fake verification.
- Build article authority pages with E-E-A-T fields.
- Add safe REST inspection endpoints for content, spam, duplicate titles, leads, and lawyers.
