## Source Limitations

- Police procedures are useful for explaining enforcement/evidence context, but they should not be treated as user-specific legal advice.
- Gov.il service pages are useful public navigation anchors, but the pages themselves state that law prevails in case of conflict.
- Knesset and research PDFs can support background/context, but final public content should verify current consolidated law before quoting exact sections, numbers or timelines.
- Marvad content involves medical privacy and licensing; it needs especially careful wording.
- Drunk-driving and testing content can overlap criminal, administrative and licensing consequences; do not flatten these into one guaranteed path.

## Global Legal Copy Rules

Every final traffic-law article must:
- avoid promising outcomes.
- avoid exact penalty, threshold, deadline or appeal claims without legal verification.
- avoid telling users to refuse, cooperate, confess, hide evidence or alter evidence.
- avoid fake ratings, fake reviews, fake badges and fake lawyer availability.
- clearly separate traffic-defense content from personal-injury road-accident compensation content.
- include a legal disclaimer.
- include a last-reviewed date.
- use official/public source links as navigation anchors, not copied text.
- keep medical/Marvad content privacy-aware and avoid medical advice.
